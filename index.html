Unit 3 Grammar: Getting Around Town

Welcome to Unit 3! In this digital whiteboard guide, we will master two key grammar structures from Our World 3: using "too" for agreeing with affirmative and negative statements, and using "but" as a contrast to connect two different ideas.

Grammar 1

"Too" for Agreeing

We use expressions with too or auxiliary verbs (do / don't / does / doesn't) to show agreement or disagreement with someone's statement.

Affirmative Agreement:
Statement: I ride my bike.
Agreement: I do, too. / Her friend does, too.

Negative Agreement / Not Agreeing:
Statement: I ride my bike.
Not agreeing: I don't. / Her friend doesn't. / I walk.

Note: don't = do not | doesn't = does not

Grammar 2

"But" as a Contrast

We use the conjunction but to connect two contrasting or opposite ideas in a single sentence.

Structure:
[First Idea], but [Second contrasting idea].

Example:
My sister takes a taxi to the mall, but my brother rides his bike.

Notice the comma before "but" when connecting two complete clauses!

Critical Reflection Questions

Think critically about how we communicate agreement and differences in our daily lives and travels around town.

Question 1

Why is it important to use words like "too" when talking with friends?

Think about how agreeing builds connections and shared experiences. When someone says they ride a bike to school, saying "I do, too" shows shared interests and empathy.

Question 2

How does the word "but" help us describe variety in a city?

Cities have many different people doing different things. The word "but" lets us compare contrasting actions—like one person taking a ferry while another takes the subway—in a single, clear sentence.

Question 3

When should you use "don't" instead of "too" in a conversation?

We use "don't" or "doesn't" when our habits or experiences are different from the other person's, allowing us to express our individuality respectfully.

Question 4

How do transportation choices affect our environment?

Comparing walking, biking, buses, and cars helps us think about sustainable travel and keeping our city clean.
